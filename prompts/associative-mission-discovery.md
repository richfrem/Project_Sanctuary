## 1. Open the associative space

Choose **3–5 substantially different domains**. Include at least two beyond computing and AI, and at least one with concrete physical, biological, artistic, historical or institutional constraints.

Use either an available mechanical sampling method or deliberate diverse selection. Report which you used. Mechanical sampling requires recorded inputs and seed; deliberate choice must not be labeled random. Do not obtain a taxonomy, build a sampler or create a domain registry solely to satisfy this exercise. Consult an existing domain ledger if present; otherwise keep selection provenance in the discovery note. Reusing a domain is allowed when the specific combination or question differs meaningfully.

Avoid letting familiar analogies dominate, but do not maintain a blanket blacklist of topics. Specificity and the quality of a connection matter more than an unusual label.

For each domain, give a short description of:
- A concrete phenomenon, practice or object.
- A mechanism, relationship or constraint.
- Something surprising, uncertain or in tension.

Initial descriptions may draw on learned knowledge. Mark uncertain details. Verify load-bearing factual claims when developing the selected idea; do not spend the exploration budget proving every preliminary association.

## 2. Explore freely before selecting

Generate a compact collection of connections, using the selected domains across the exercise. Around 6–10 short crossings is a useful ceiling, not a quota. Stop earlier if a rich direction emerges.

Try several moves:
- Transfer a mechanism or formal relationship into a different setting.
- Reverse, impose or remove a constraint.
- Change the representation: turn a process into a spatial design, a musical structure, a protocol or another concrete form.
- Explore a deceptive similarity: use where the analogy fails to generate a better question.
- Combine contrasts rather than similarities.

Develop one or two promising crossings into a second generation of ideas. Introduce a third domain where it contributes something. Give at least one initially awkward idea a fair development before dismissing it.

During this phase, ideas may remain provisional. Do not demand a quantitative model, control group, literature claim or immediate utility before an idea has developed. Do not present speculation as fact.

Preserve a concise discovery note: selected domains, a few crossings and their descendants, surprises, and selection decisions. Record authored summaries and observable artifacts; no hidden chain-of-thought transcript or claims of access to training weights are requested.

## 3. Select one seed for a mission

Develop **up to three** distinct candidate briefs. Fewer are acceptable; do not manufacture survivors. For each, briefly state:
- The question or possible artifact and its contributing domains.
- What attracts your interest and what remains uncertain.
- What the association changes: the question, prediction, method, design or interpretation.
- An affordable next step and the main risk or missing prerequisite.

A connection may generate a valuable question even if it is unnecessary to the final explanation. Preserve that as generative provenance. Shared vocabulary alone is not evidence of a shared mechanism.

Select using interest, learning potential, productive tension and feasibility. Do not reject an idea merely because it needs research or elaboration: that development can be the mission’s first phase. Feasibility means there is an affordable route toward a meaningful result, not that the answer is already known.

Perform a targeted related-mission and prior-work check. Differentiate “new here,” “a distinct framing relative to sources inspected,” and broader originality. No search establishes exhaustive novelty. If the question is already answered, consider a replication, a boundary case, a creative reinterpretation or another candidate. Describe the contribution honestly.

If no viable seed emerges, allow one bounded return to exploration if resources remain. Otherwise preserve the discovery outcome and stop honestly. Do not create a fictitious research hypothesis or an unsupported registry status merely to count a mission as completed.

## 4. Register the mission and give the idea room to develop

Once a seed is selected, use the next available ID and existing mission-registration workflow. Use the canonical schema, supported commands, justified bidirectional links and declared impact. Do not guess CLI flags or invent schema values. Obtain a task-owned lease/workspace before edits when required.

Record:
- Interest statement and the associative origin of the question.
- Strategic intent, expected learning and limits of the current idea.
- Canonical scoring breakdown and calculated priority, labeled as estimates.
- Readiness, available capabilities, missing prerequisites and first enabling step.
- Scope, budget, stop conditions and intended artifacts.

Register at the stage the work has actually reached. Do not confuse registration, lease ownership, completed execution or empirical corroboration. If the mission requires development before a confirmatory hypothesis is defensible, state that explicitly using schema-supported fields or a linked design note. Never invent thresholds solely to fill a field; use the existing workflow to resolve a schema constraint.

The mission may include these phases:
1. **Elaboration:** sharpen the idea, its mapping and possible outputs.
2. **Research:** inspect relevant sources and existing methods; verify load-bearing claims and record reading depth.
3. **Development:** construct instruments, prototypes or a creative brief and perform disclosed pilot/calibration work.
4. **Investigation or creation:** run the appropriate method within scope.
5. **Assessment and preservation:** record what was learned or made and complete delivery.

Research may reveal a different useful framing. Record changes before further execution and distinguish exploratory development from confirmatory testing. Material scope/budget changes or a stopped frozen run require applicable authorization. Do not spawn another mission for each refinement.

## 5. Choose validation appropriate to the claim

### Empirical or analytical investigation

Before confirmatory execution, specify and freeze through Sanctuary’s existing process:
- A precise hypothesis and the bounded claim the method can assess.
- Inputs, instruments, baseline, metrics, thresholds, coverage and stop rules.
- Which assumptions are sourced, transferred or introduced for this inquiry.
- Possible outcomes that would contradict the hypothesis.
- Required instrument checks and separation of development from held-out cases where applicable.

If claiming that the **cross-domain transfer improves an outcome**, include a meaningful comparison without the transferred component and, where useful, a matched substitute. Match resources and complexity where possible. Test what the transfer actually contributes; arbitrary domain replacement is not automatically a valid control.

If the association only inspired the question, do not require an ablation that has no bearing on the actual hypothesis. Test the question on its own merits and describe the association as provenance.

Check units, scales and boundary conditions for quantitative mappings. A simulation encoding a mechanism can demonstrate consequences under its assumptions; it does not independently establish the mechanism in nature. Exhaustive finite computation establishes the specified finite result, not an unrestricted theorem.

Commit and verify required freezes before held-out execution. Keep the original checkpoint intact, with later corrections or amendments separately recorded. Reference a freeze commit from subsequent records; do not attempt to embed a commit’s own hash into itself.

### Creative deliverable

Define a concrete brief, material/formal constraints and observable completion criteria. Identify how the contributing domains shape the work. A removal or substitution exercise may illuminate the contribution, but need not mechanically validate artistic worth.

Separate completion, craft quality, originality and personal expression. Use a bounded review when valuable; label self-review honestly. Creative work need not imitate a scientific experiment. Empirical claims about its effects still need appropriate evidence.

### Review and predictions

Record a small number of useful expectations before execution, with optional subjective confidence. Do not claim confidence calibration without evidence.

Use independent review where consequential mistakes are plausible and the budget supports it. Prefer a different model family when available. Supply the actual protocol, code or artifact and a narrow review question. Preserve failures and cost for all attempts. Reviewer agreement is not ground truth.

## 6. Execute, reflect and assess

Use deterministic computation and existing capabilities where appropriate. Preserve relevant scripts, inputs, versions, outputs, sources and failures. Keep exploratory follow-ups distinct from frozen tests.

Assess against the actual registered criteria. An informative falsification is valuable learning. Incomplete evidence, instrument failure and a disproved hypothesis are different outcomes; use only supported registry values. Do not write an empirical verdict when the evidence does not justify one.

Keep these dimensions separate in the report; they are descriptive labels, not new schema enums:
- Execution completeness.
- Scientific assessment, where applicable.
- Evidence that the transfer itself contributed, if that claim was tested.
- Creative deliverable completion, where applicable.
- Integration, local capture and remote preservation.

Optionally reflect: Did the inquiry change an assumption, suggest another domain connection or reveal a useful unresolved tension? Record evidence and where the analogy breaks down. “No clear connection yet” is acceptable. Do not force operational usefulness or automatically create more WIP.
