# arXiv analysis

## Relevance to existing missions

1. **On Agentic Manipulation Policies (2611.09892v1)**: none. Robot manipulation with VLAs and WAMs is outside every Sanctuary mission's scope.

2. **CPU-ARC: Agentic Reasoning Caching for on-CPU Reasoning Reuse (2611.09891v1)**: MISSION-012 (selective forgetting and memory compression) and MISSION-008 (cognitive continuity checkpoints). The abstract says LLMs re-solve problems instead of reusing solutions, which bears on caching reasoning traces. It challenges the assumption that fresh solving is needed, but gives no evidence on this.

3. **On the Reliability of Majority-Vote Pseudo-Labels in TTRL (2611.09872v1)**: MISSION-009 and MISSION-004 (independent-first reasoning against majority pressure). The abstract decomposes majority-vote error into population-majority bias and prompt-dependent variation, which is a relevant framing for consensus-driven errors, though TTRL itself is not directly testable here.

4. **GRAC: Graph-Based Retrieval-Augmented Captioning for Surgical Scene Description (2611.09866v1)**: none. The domain is surgical documentation.

5. **Grasp-TAGS (2611.09864v1)**: none. The domain is robot loco-manipulation.

## Impact on the project

none. The abstracts give no architectural, skill, or practice change that would justify action. The CPU-ARC idea of caching reasoning for reuse is worth noting for memory design, but it is not yet a concrete change.

## Improvement ideas

1. When a mission's hypothesis is built on majority or consensus agreement, record the agreement rate alongside the verdict, so majority-driven errors are visible (suggested by 2611.09872v1).
2. Keep a log of solved sub-problems keyed by problem signature, so repeated tasks can check the log before re-deriving (suggested by 2611.09891v1).

## New mission seeds

1. **Does reusing cached reasoning traces match fresh solving on repeated tasks?** Choose 10 repeated tasks from existing mission logs, solve each once fresh and once with a cached trace, and compare accuracy and token cost.
   - First step: list repeated task types in the mission history and count how many recur.

2. **Does majority-vote agreement predict correctness in Sanctuary's own multi-sample checks?** Take a set of past multi-sample decisions, record agreement levels, and check whether high agreement correlates with correct outcomes.
   - First step: pull five past decisions that used multi-sample agreement and tabulate their agreement rates against their verified results.

Note: every point above is based on abstracts only. No full text was read, so no claim here has been verified against the papers' methods or results.
