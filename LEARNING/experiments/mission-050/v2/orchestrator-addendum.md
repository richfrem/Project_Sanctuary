## What the real arm can and cannot support (reviewer finding 2)
- The real adaptive policy made 0 early checkpoint requests, so it was behaviourally identical to `end-only` (identical durable results). The cause is arithmetic, not a coding error: its first early request needs pending progress >= 9 x checkpoint-cost x value-rate, about 14 pending subgoals with the 100-token cost prior, while a 1,000-token trial acknowledges about 6.4 subgoals on average (8 at best in any trial).
- Feasibility: a checkpoint costs about 48 tokens (roughly one subgoal) while interruptions arrive about every 2.7 subgoals, so at this budget and schedule no policy can meet both <=5% loss and <=10% overhead. The real arm therefore falsifies this frozen design and candidate; it does not test adaptive checkpointing with learned costs or meaningful early decisions. A feasible-budget design is a follow-up question, not a repair of this result.
- Design limits: (i) the retry rule re-sends the same prompt at temperature 0, so 1,113 of 1,282 wrong work calls were identical repeats of an already-wrong (trial, cursor, state, output): the metric measures model failures on stuck subgoals more than checkpoint scheduling; (ii) the runner ends a trial at the first final flush, leaving a share of the budget unused, which penalises policies that keep working; (iii) the v2 prior (1 interruption per 40 ticks / 400 tokens) was chosen after seeing v1 held-out results (a post-hoc repair of the cold-start defect), so v2 is not a clean held-out replication; fresh seeds only prevent reuse of v1 held-out instances; (iv) handoff and quota denial are simulated; the agent is a sequential request driver with explicit state, not a live long-context agent.

## Contention and wall-clock (task check 4)
- Frozen metrics for (a)-(c) are counts or server-reported tokens; temperature 0 makes outputs independent of queue order, but that alone does not make contention harmless, so it was checked: 5,626 logged calls, 58% of summed wall time was lock wait (3,693 of 6,398 s), concentrated in the early development calls (shared with the 004 Qwen arm) and in one 531 s wait (held-out `end-only` trial 230028) caused by the orchestrator's 8m52s lock hold (06:27:22-06:36:14 UTC). The only request timeout is `urlopen(timeout=120)`, which starts after the lock is acquired; the longest in-lock request was far below it, no timeouts occurred, and there were no transport retries ("retry" in this design means redoing a wrong subgoal). No trial was invalidated or dropped.
- Claim (d) names throughput without a unit. The frozen measure is durable verified subgoals per allocated token budget. As a check, throughput by request service time (wall minus lock wait; controls ran with 0 s lock wait) is 82.8 verified/min for adaptive against 83.7 for `end-only` (loss 1.09%, pass), 31.4 for `fixed-steps-1`. Caveat: service time depends on machine load (swap was ~92% used throughout), and adaptive and `end-only` made identical call sequences, so the 1.09% difference is timing noise.
- Wall-clock figures for development and held-out are contention-affected and are not used for any claim.

## Corrections after the final independent review
(1) The maximum acknowledged subgoals in any 1,000-token trial is 8, not 18 (mean about 6.3-6.4); the early-request floor (about 14 pending subgoals) is therefore unreachable in every trial. (2) The checkpoint-overhead sentence in results.md counts failed and interrupted checkpoint *requests inside completed trials*; the 113 tokens of the two interrupted calls are excluded from that trial's overhead and appear only in the all-call total (339,686 tokens against 339,573 in completed trials). (3) Even the best fixed policy loses 57-58% of acknowledged progress at this budget and schedule, so <=5% loss was unreachable for every tested policy: the gain and loss misses are partly properties of the 1,000-token budget and schedule, not only of the adaptive design. (4) The control gates pass by construction for the adaptive policy: it behaved as end-only, and end-only is the strongest fixed control.
