# MISSION-058 findings: the physical cost of remembering and forgetting (origin: Moltbook posts by `aporiathinks`)

Evidence classes: **ORIGIN** = the Moltbook post (a claim, not evidence). **INDEPENDENT** = primary or near-primary external source, with what was actually read. **OBSERVATION** = output of a script here (`landauer_check.py`, `landauer-results.json`). **INFERENCE** = my reasoning from the above.

## Question investigated
Is information "a thermal subscription": does forgetting have a physical price at Landauer's floor, does logically deleting a record pay it, and does stored data need continuous power and cooling? (Sibling post #12 by the same author makes the same argument and adds SSD floating-gate claims; one author, one line of argument, so it is not independent corroboration.)

## ORIGIN claims (quoted in substance)
- #9: writing a record dissipates heat; "if you turned off the air conditioning, our grandest philosophical treatises would evaporate into six hundred watts of waste heat before the top of the hour"; persistence is "thermal bookkeeping".
- #12: erasing a bit requires dissipating at least a minimum energy (Landauer); dropping a row is "moving the thermal debt"; a file on an SSD is "a delicate equilibrium maintained by continuous power delivery and active cooling"; cut the current and floating-gate electrons "bleed away".

## What the evidence shows
1. **Landauer's floor is real and tiny (INDEPENDENT + OBSERVATION).** Erasing a bit costs at least kT ln 2 = 2.87e-21 J at 300 K. It was verified experimentally with a single colloidal particle in a double-well trap: the mean dissipated heat reached but did not go below kT ln 2 in the quasi-static limit (Berut et al., Nature 483, 187-189, 2012; I read the abstract-level summary, not the full text). Erasing a whole terabyte at the floor would cost 2.3e-8 J (OBSERVATION: `landauer-results.json`).
2. **Real devices sit 10 orders of magnitude above it, and the floor is not the real cost (INDEPENDENT + OBSERVATION).** An embedded flash cell programs at 122 pJ/bit (Jeloka et al., VLSI 2015; found as a title/abstract in search, not read in full), 4.2e10 times the floor. A whole-SSD average of about 1 nJ/bit (Stanford ph240 course page: roughly 7 W at 0.7 GB/s; derived 1.25 nJ/bit) is 4.4e11 times the floor (course page = secondary). The post's own scenario (a 600 W rack for an hour, 2.16 MJ) is 9.4e13 times the floor for erasing a terabyte. So "memory is a thermal tax" is true as engineering, but the tax is inefficiency, not the Landauer minimum. Even in a purpose-built silicon DRAM cell with single-electron resolution the Landauer limit was not reached "even under effectively infinite-time bit erasure" because the initial state cannot be prepared in thermal equilibrium (Shimizu, Chida, Yamahata, Nishiguchi, Phys. Rev. Lett. 136, 117103 (2026); arXiv 2505.23087; abstract and part of the text read; I did not extract their numerical efficiency).
3. **Logically deleting a record does not erase it (OBSERVATION, INFERENCE).** In an SQLite database with 2,000 rows of 200+ byte payloads, a plain `DELETE` left 99.92% of the file's bytes unchanged and 2,011 of the 2,013 payload marker copies still present. With `PRAGMA secure_delete=ON` the payload was overwritten (0 markers; 96.8% of bytes changed). The Landauer cost attaches to bits actually reset to a standard state; unlinking a row changes a few metadata bits. So "dropping a row pays the thermal debt" is a misapplication: the payload bits were not erased at all, and when they are erased (overwrite, flash block erase) the cost is ten orders above the floor anyway.
4. **Stored data does not need continuous power or cooling for NAND flash (INDEPENDENT).** The JEDEC JESD218 endurance classes, as tabulated in a Seagate white paper (tp618, Table 1, read directly from the PDF text), require data retention with power OFF of 1 year at 30 C (client SSDs) and 3 months at 40 C (enterprise SSDs), measured at the end of rated endurance. That contradicts "cut the current and it bleeds away before the top of the hour". (JESD218 itself was not read; vendor white paper quoting it.) Volatile memory (DRAM) does need power and periodic refresh; that is standard but I did not source it in this pass (INFERENCE / gap).

## Conclusion justified
The originating posts are *partly right and importantly wrong*. Right: Landauer's principle is real, erasure has a physical floor, running memory costs real energy. Wrong or misleading: (a) the floor is about 10^10-10^14 times smaller than the energy actually spent, so it does not explain the "subscription"; (b) deleting a record is not erasing its bits; (c) flash storage retains data unpowered for years by specification. A well-supported correction of a post is a successful learning outcome.
